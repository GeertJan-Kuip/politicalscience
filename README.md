In 2020 I presented a paper named _'Migration and Liberal Democracy: The political preferences of those who leave and those who stay behind'_ at an online conference at Radboud University Nijmegen.  My co-author was Mark Dechesne, political scientist at Leiden University, who had been a great mentor and who had encouraged me to apply. Many of the participants in my session were PhD students in the field of Political Science. The presentation went well, especially my [3D presentation of research data](https://geertjan-kuip.github.io/politicologenetmaal-2020-data-visualisation/) was received favorably. 

While I have never taken the effort to get the article published, I am extremely happy to have worked on it and to have been able to get it in front of peers. During the process I learned a lot, like:

- How to get to the bottom of an academic field (personality psychology in my case)
- How to write clear, non-ambiguous academic texts
- How to get value from large, publicly available datasets
- How to structure a research article
- How terrible an idea it is to rely on statistical software without understanding the underlying fundamentals

Since the presentation I have worked hard to gain a better understanding of the field of statistics, which I managed to do. Eventually I returned to the more technical problems of data and software. It was a great experience, and if you are interested in the article you are welcome to read it.
